Overview

Background

Effective malaria control depends on the timely, accurate linkage of surveillance data (suspected fever, confirmed malaria and treatment of confirmed malaria cases) with commodity consumption data (RDTs used and ACTs dispensed), and visibility of stock levels at facility and district level. This workshop brings together surveillance, case management, and supply-chain staff to align these indicators, build minimum/maximum stock-level and reorder logic into routine reporting, and configure epidemic threshold alerts within eIDSR.

Overall objectives

  1. Strengthen the linkage between suspected fever cases tested and RDTs consumed, and between confirmed malaria cases and ACTs dispensed, within routine facility and district reporting.
  2. Build participant capacity to compute Average Monthly Consumption (AMC), Minimum Stock Level, Maximum Stock Level, Reorder Level and Safety Stock for RDTs and ACTs.
  3. Configure and/or reinforce use of the eIDSR platform to capture, validate, and visualize malaria commodity and case-based indicators in real time.
  4. Enable automated/semi-automated stock alerts within eIDSR to trigger timely replenishment and mitigate stockouts.
  5. Build capacity to define, set, and interpret malaria epidemic thresholds (alert and epidemic/action thresholds) using historical facility/district data.
  6. Produce facility- and district-level action plans, including SOPs for threshold breaches and stock alerts.

Target participants

  • National Surveillance Officers
  • Malaria Epidemiology Surveillance Monitoring and Evaluation Officers
  • Pharmacists/Stores/Logistics Officers
  • IT/Data Scientist Specialists
  • eIDSR data managers

The five days

Key indicators covered

Testing Rate(Number of RDTs used ÷ Number of suspected fever cases seen) × 100
Test Positivity Rate(Confirmed malaria cases ÷ Number tested) × 100
ACT Utilization Ratio(Number of ACTs dispensed ÷ Number of confirmed malaria cases) × 100
Stock measuresStock on Hand, Average Monthly Consumption (AMC), Minimum Stock Level, Maximum Stock Level, Reorder Level, Months of Stock (MOS)
ThresholdsAlert Threshold and Epidemic/Action Threshold for malaria case trends

Outputs and logistics

Expected outputs of the workshop

  1. A shared set of aligned indicators linking fever testing, confirmed cases, RDT/ACT consumption and stock status.
  2. Facility-level Minimum/Maximum stock levels and Reorder Levels computed for RDTs and ACTs.
  3. eIDSR configured (or a documented configuration plan) with stock alert triggers and malaria epidemic thresholds.
  4. Draft SOPs for responding to low-stock alerts and to epidemic threshold breaches.
  5. Facility/district action plans with clear roles, responsibilities and timelines.

Logistics notes

  • Participants should bring at least 10 years of facility fever-case, RDT, confirmed-case and ACT consumption data (paper or electronic) for practical sessions on Days 2 to 5.
  • A laptop/tablet with eIDSR platform access (or demo credentials) is required for Day 4 and Day 5 practical sessions.
  • Daily sessions run 08:30 to 16:00/16:45 with a mid-morning break, lunch, and a mid-afternoon break as needed.

Presentation outline

03

Background

1

Malaria transmission in Sierra Leone is perennial, accounting for 40% of clinical consultations.

2

Health workers diagnose suspected malaria cases using rapid diagnostic tests (RDT) and microscopy, with facility-level results reported to the Health Management Information System (HMIS) as monthly aggregates.

3

Antimalarial commodity stocks (RDTS, ACTs, ITNs, SP etc) are reported to the Logistics Management Information System (LMIS).

4

NMCP needs to use eIDSR weekly malaria data to track malaria commodities because weekly surveillance is the earliest, most sensitive signal of commodity pressure.

5

DHIS2 monthly LMIS data alone is too slow to prevent stockouts in high-burden or hard-to-reach districts/chiefdoms.

04

Weekly eIDSR data detects sudden increases in malaria cases

Monthly LMIS reporting shows consumption after 30 days, but malaria case increases happen within days. Weekly eIDSR data allows NMCP to see:

  • Sharp increases in suspected malaria
  • Rising test positivity
  • Increased confirmed cases
  • Increased ACT consumption pressure
This early warning helps NMCP anticipate where RDTs and ACTs will run out before the monthly LMIS report arrives.
05

Malaria case trends to predict commodity consumption

RDT and ACT consumption are directly linked to:

  • Number of suspected cases (RDT use)
  • Number of confirmed cases (ACT use)

When eIDSR shows a spike in cases:

  • RDT use will increase immediately
  • ACT use will increase within 1 to 3 days
Weekly data allows NMCP to forecast consumption and trigger emergency resupply.
06

Preventing stockouts especially in hard-to-reach areas

Districts like Bonthe, Moyamba, Port Loko, Kambia and riverine PHUs face:

  • Boat-dependent transport
  • Delayed deliveries
  • Limited buffer stock capacity

Monthly LMIS data is too slow for these areas. Weekly eIDSR data enables:

  • Early identification of PHUs at risk
  • Faster mobilization of emergency stocks
  • Better planning of boat logistics
07

eIDSR data to validate LMIS consumption

LMIS sometimes shows:

  • Under-reported consumption
  • Over-reported closing balances
  • Missing reports
  • Delayed submissions

Weekly eIDSR data helps NMCP detect inconsistencies:

High cases + low RDT use→under-reporting
Low cases + high ACT use→irrational use
High cases + zero ACT use→stockout
This improves data quality and supply chain accuracy.
08

Weekly surveillance supports outbreak detection

Malaria outbreaks begin with:

  • eIDSR detects these Rising fever cases
  • Rising test positivity
  • Rising confirmed malaria cases

eIDSR detects patterns weeks earlier than DHIS2 LMIS. Early detection allows NMCP to:

  • Pre-position RDTs and ACTs
  • Activate Rapid Response Teams
  • Prevent facility-level stockouts during outbreaks
09

Weekly surveillance for rapid commodity response

NMCP can use weekly eIDSR trends to:

  • Adjust buffer stock levels
  • Prioritize high-burden PHUs
  • Plan distribution routes
  • Allocate commodities based on real-time burden
This is essential for districts with seasonal spikes (e.g., rainy season peaks).
10

eIDSR data for accountability and targeted supervision

Supervisors can use weekly trends to:

  • Identify PHUs with unusual patterns
  • Target facilities for supervision
  • Verify stock cards and registers
  • Ensure correct RDT/ACT use
This strengthens both surveillance and supply chain governance.
